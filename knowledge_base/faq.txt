SOLAR FAQ

Q: What is solar energy?

A: Solar energy is energy obtained from sunlight and can be converted into electricity using photovoltaic solar panels.

Q: What is a solar panel?

A: A solar panel is a photovoltaic device that converts sunlight into electrical energy.

Q: What is a solar inverter?

A: A solar inverter converts DC electricity from solar panels or batteries into AC electricity used by many electrical appliances.

Q: What are the main types of solar systems?

A: The main types are on-grid, off-grid, and hybrid systems.

Q: What is an on-grid system?

A: An on-grid system is connected to the utility electricity grid.

Q: What is an off-grid system?

A: An off-grid system operates independently of the utility grid and normally requires battery storage.

Q: What is a hybrid system?

A: A hybrid system combines solar generation, battery storage, and grid interaction depending on the system design.

Q: Do solar panels work at night?

A: Solar panels require sunlight to generate electricity, so they do not normally generate useful electricity at night. Stored battery energy or grid electricity can be used instead.

Q: Do solar panels work on cloudy days?

A: Solar panels can generate electricity during cloudy conditions, but output is generally lower than under strong direct sunlight.

Q: How many solar panels do I need?

A: The required number depends on electricity consumption, solar resource, panel wattage, system losses, available area, and system design.

Q: Do I need batteries?

A: Batteries are generally important for off-grid systems and can provide backup or energy storage in hybrid systems. They are not necessarily required for basic on-grid systems.

Q: What is battery capacity?

A: Battery capacity represents the amount of electrical energy that a battery can store, commonly expressed in kWh.

Q: How long can a battery provide backup?

A: Backup duration depends on battery usable energy and the power consumed by the connected loads.

Q: Which solar panel is best?

A: The appropriate panel depends on efficiency, wattage, quality, warranty, available space, budget, and system requirements.

Q: Which is better, monocrystalline or polycrystalline?

A: Modern monocrystalline panels generally offer higher efficiency, while polycrystalline panels can provide a cost-effective option depending on the application.

Q: How long do solar panels last?

A: Solar panels are designed for long-term operation, but their output gradually decreases over time. Actual service life depends on product quality, environmental conditions, and maintenance.

Q: What affects solar panel output?

A: Sunlight, shading, temperature, orientation, tilt, dirt, installation quality, and system losses can affect output.

Q: How is solar system size estimated?

A: Basic estimation uses daily energy consumption, available solar resource, system losses, and equipment characteristics.

Q: Can solar provide backup during a power outage?

A: Backup depends on the system design. Standard grid-connected systems may shut down during grid outages, while appropriately designed hybrid or off-grid systems can provide backup.

Q: Why is my solar production lower than expected?

A: Possible causes include shading, dirt, high temperature, equipment problems, incorrect configuration, seasonal changes, or other system losses.

Q: Can I run an air conditioner on solar?

A: Yes, but the solar system must be appropriately sized for the air conditioner's running power, starting requirements, operating hours, and other loads.

Q: Can solar reduce my electricity bill?

A: A properly designed solar system can reduce electricity purchased from the grid, but savings depend on energy consumption, system size, solar generation, electricity tariffs, and applicable policies.
